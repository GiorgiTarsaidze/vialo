# Judge-first-impression review

## Five-second landing test

A new viewer should understand that they describe one day and receive a schedule that fits. The first viewport must provide one input, one action, and a concrete outcome promise without model or pipeline jargon.

## Four-second result test

The result must immediately communicate the savings, the optimized route, and the feasible scheduled day. The comparison is the proof; the timeline is the product.

## Six-minute demo test

1. Prompt entry is obvious and fast.
2. Loading describes real pipeline stages without fake percentages.
3. The route comparison lands as the visual reveal.
4. Timeline evidence demonstrates opening-hour and travel constraints.
5. An infeasible example explains a dropped stop honestly.
6. Google Maps handoff and sharing complete the story.
7. Kiro artifacts and captured workflow evidence show spec-driven development.

Remove anything that delays this story. Record confusion points, dead time, weak proof, and visual distractions in order of impact on judging.
